A Linguistically-Informed Annotation Strategy for Korean Semantic Role Labeling

Korean semantic role labeling data converted from the Sejong verb dictionary into CoNLL-style format

LREC-COLING 2024

Yige Chen1, KyungTae Lim2,‡, Jungyeul Park3

1The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong 2SeoulTech, South Korea 3The University of British Columbia, Canada

‡Corresponding author

Paper (ACL Anthology) PDF

Abstract

Semantic role labeling is an essential component of semantic and syntactic processing of natural languages, which reveals the predicate-argument structure of the language. Despite its importance, semantic role labeling for the Korean language has not been studied extensively. One notable issue is the lack of uniformity among data annotation strategies across different datasets, which often lack thorough rationales. In this study, we suggest an annotation strategy for Korean semantic role labeling that is in line with the previously proposed linguistic theories as well as the distinct properties of the Korean language. We further propose a simple yet viable conversion strategy from the Sejong verb dictionary to a CoNLL-style dataset for Korean semantic role labeling. Experiment results using a transformer-based sequence labeling model demonstrate the reliability and trainability of the converted dataset.

Keywords Korean semantic role labeling (SRL) Predicate-argument structure Arguments vs. modifiers Sejong verb dictionary CoNLL-style dataset Korean PropBank Case-marking postpositions Annotation scheme Korean NLP

Annotation Strategy and Conversion

Korean is an agglutinative, subject-object-verb (SOV) language in which postpositions attached to a word usually mark its case (e.g. 이/가 i/ga nominative, 을/를 (l-)eul accusative, 에서 eseo locative/ablative). Following Categorial Grammar, the paper defines arguments as syntactically mandatory and semantically necessary constituents that bear case and appear in the predicate's subcategorization frame; every other constituent is a modifier. Two consequences for annotation:

  • An argument may span several words (eojeol), which the word-level Korean PropBank SRL data (about 186,300 tokens) cannot represent.
  • The case-marking postposition belongs to the argument, whereas the NIKL SRL data (about 2,000,000 tokens) label only the lexical morphemes.

The Sejong verb dictionary is then converted into a CoNLL-style SRL dataset:

  1. Morphological analysis. Example sentences are POS-tagged with a morpheme-level tagger and merged back into word-level tokens.
  2. Dependency parsing. Stanza adds heads and dependency relations to each word.
  3. Chunking. The segment before the target verb is split into candidate argument chunks, closing a chunk whenever a token ends with a postposition (according to its XPOS tag).
  4. Chunk-frame alignment. Each argument in the subcategorization frame is assigned, left to right, to the first unlabeled chunk carrying the same postposition. Labels use the BIO scheme with argument indices 0 to 3 inherited from the frame.

Sentences with null (phonologically covert) postpositions or with the auxiliary postposition 도 (do, ‘as well’), which replaces the case marker, cannot be aligned and are removed from the final dataset. Main Results

  • Trainable converted data. A KoELECTRA-Base-v3 sequence labeler trained on 20,437 converted sentences (2-fold cross-validation) reaches exact-match precision 0.946 ± 0.003, recall 0.971 ± 0.002 and F1 0.954 ± 0.003.
  • Multi-word arguments. Arguments are annotated as BIO spans over chunks, so an argument can cover several eojeol, unlike the word-level Korean PropBank SRL dataset.
  • Case-bearing arguments. The postposition that carries the case is kept inside the argument span, unlike the NIKL SRL annotation that labels only the lexical part.
